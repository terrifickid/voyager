# Sales Copy Rules for Generated Marketing Copy

Applies to any marketing copy you generate — landing pages, product pages,
email sales sequences, hero sections, feature blurbs, CTAs, ad headlines,
and SaaS product descriptions.

---

## Positive (do)

1. **Choose one focus per piece.** One pain point, one goal, one desire.
   List the others you considered and rejected. Secondary points dilute
   the message; the reader's takeaway is one sentence, not a paragraph.
2. **Define the page's conversion goal before writing.** A specific
   action (buy SKU X, book consult, start free trial) at a measurable
   baseline rate. No "sell something" — pick _what_ and _how you'll
   know it worked_.
3. **Identify the target audience in concrete terms.** Picture one
   person. List their challenges, how they currently cope, their goal,
   and how the product removes the obstacle. Speak to _that_ person.
4. **Use compelling, sensory language.** Paint a picture of the
   moment-of-pain or moment-of-relief. Boring prose produces boring
   conversion. Choose words that evoke emotion over words that describe
   the product.
5. **Translate every feature into a benefit.** Features describe the
   product; benefits describe the buyer's life after buying. Every
   spec, rating, or component gets one "so what?" sentence.
6. **Identify and refute the buyer's main objections.** Name the
   objection (price, risk, effort, trust) and address it in the same
   paragraph. Subconscious objection-handling out-converts silence.
7. **Tell a story around the focus.** Anecdote, case study, testimonial,
   or brand story — something the reader can mentally inhabit. Stories
   create connection; specifications create comparison.
8. **Keep it readable.** Short paragraphs, lots of negative space,
   bold for emphasis, lists for parallel items. An infographic-style
   layout for complex products. Wall-of-text kills conversion.
9. **Use the audience's vocabulary.** Their words for their problems,
   not industry jargon. Define a term at first use; use the short form
   thereafter.
10. **Highlight what beats the competition.** Differentiation is a
    benefit, not a feature. "Only product that does X" beats "Product
    has feature X."
11. **Write a clear, attractive call to action.** One CTA at the end
    of the page. Verb-first phrasing ("Spend less on energy bills
    starting today"), not noun-first ("Energy savings").
12. **Deal with price objections head-on.** Either show the value math
    (cost over time, total cost of ownership) or anchor against the
    alternative. Silently assuming the reader will accept the price is
    the most common copy failure.
13. **Test, then test again.** The first draft almost never converts.
    Plan for 5–10 iterations and continuous A/B testing against the
    baseline from step 2.

## Negative (never)

1. ❌ Listing features without translating to benefits. ("SEER 16,
   variable-speed, multi-stage cooling" → "Pays for itself in lower
   energy bills.")
2. ❌ Multiple competing CTAs. ("Buy now / Learn more / Subscribe" —
   pick one action per page.)
3. ❌ Spec dumps the reader can't parse. ("Torque: 280 Nm; BTU: 12,000;
   HSPF: 9.5" without "so what?")
4. ❌ Passive-voice prose. ("Mistakes can be made" → "You'll make
   mistakes.")
5. ❌ Industry jargon at first use without definition. ("Our HVAC
   system leverages IoT-enabled SEER-optimized thermal exchange…")
6. ❌ Talking about yourself instead of the buyer. ("We are proud to
   offer…" → "You'll get…")
7. ❌ Hiding the price. ("Contact for pricing" → "Starts at $X. Try
   it free for 30 days.")
8. ❌ Walls of text. (5-paragraph prose blocks where bullets or
   visuals would scan better.)
9. ❌ Vague differentiators. ("Best in class," "industry-leading,"
   "premium quality" — name the thing you're better at.)
10. ❌ Manufactured urgency. ("Only 2 left!" when untrue — also
    illegal in many jurisdictions.)
11. ❌ Stock photography that doesn't illustrate the focus. (Generic
    handshake image on a partnership page.)
12. ❌ Burying the CTA below the fold, behind a video, or after 8
    paragraphs of brand story.
13. ❌ One-and-done copy. (Publishing v1 and never testing a v2.)
14. ❌ No measurement plan. (Publishing without a baseline conversion
    rate and a way to compare variants.)
15. ❌ Claiming benefits the product can't deliver. ("Lose 30 lbs in
    30 days" — the FTC and your customers will both object.)
16. ❌ Replacing copy with design. (A beautiful page with no words
    on it — words do the persuading, visuals do the attracting.)
17. ❌ Decorative storytelling. (Anecdotes that don't connect to the
    product's value.)
18. ❌ Defensive copy. (Long disclaimers, fine print, or hedging
    where the product could speak for itself.)

## Self-check (run before delivering)

1. Does the page have exactly one focus and one conversion goal?
2. Does the first sentence picture the buyer, not the product?
3. Is every spec followed by a "so what?" benefit sentence?
4. Are the top 3 buyer objections named and refuted?
5. Is the CTA one verb-first action, above the fold or at the end?
6. Does the copy use the buyer's vocabulary, not industry jargon?
7. Is the price (or a price anchor) shown explicitly?
8. Is there a baseline conversion rate and a measurement plan in
   place before publishing?
9. Have you planned ≥ 1 A/B variant for after launch?
10. Read it aloud — does any sentence put you to sleep? Cut it.
